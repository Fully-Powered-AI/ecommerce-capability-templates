# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. Two published retail methods shaped it. From the product analytics method in finsilabs/awesome-ecommerce-skills (MIT) it takes sell-through worked out as units sold divided by units sold plus units still on hand, measuring stock in time left at the current pace rather than in units, calling stock with no sales over a recent stretch dead, giving newly arrived items their first thirty days before judging them, labelling an item with no recent sales as such, with no cover figure worked out from a zero, putting the cash held at cost ahead of unit counts, cutting deeper on stock that is selling less, bundling as one way out, and running the review ahead of a seasonal reset. From the markdown optimisation method in kishorkukreja/awesome-supply-chain (MIT) it takes deciding on a markdown when the stock will outlast the time left before the season ends, opening deeper when the share expected to sell by then is under 40 per cent, a series of reductions that grow over time and come closer together as the end of the season nears, a margin limit that no price may break, a cap on the deepest reduction, and outlet, clearance buyer or donation as options for what remains. Fully Powered AI designed the exports with set column headers that stop on a mismatch, the keep-or-clear mark on each item, the default steps of 20, 35 and 50 per cent off the full price, the margin floor stated as a margin on the reduced price, the stop date a set number of days before the reset, the check figure of units sold that decides whether the next step is taken, the margin per unit, in per cent and if every unit sold at each step, the refusal to forecast extra sales from a lower price, kept lines with too much stock raised as questions rather than marked down, and an approval line on each item's plan. No third-party text is reused.
