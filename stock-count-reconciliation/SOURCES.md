Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. One published method shaped it: the QuickBooks Online stock reconciliation method in skills-agents-co/skills-and-agents-library (MIT), in its finance-team folder. From it come comparing a count with the books item by item; working without changing the books; the care needed when a count is made part-way through a day; settling the counting unit, the locations covered and what a missing item means before any comparison; keeping the comparison to the locations actually counted, never setting a count of some locations against books for the whole business or reading that gap as a loss; stopping on unusable count rows; confirming against a separate list of tracked items before concluding there is no stock, and never reading a failed report as no stock; reading a tracked item that a report leaves out as zero; a unit cost only where the books hold units, with values given as average-cost estimates; a book value with no units behind it raised as a question; items found on one side only; a net total that leaves out what cannot be valued; and a first reading of why an item is short or over. Fully Powered AI rewrote the method for any accounting or stock system and designed the rest: the default export headers with a stop on a mismatch; differences explained from receiving, returns and damage notes not yet entered; the ordered checks for each shortfall and surplus, with unexplained loss last and no person named; equal and opposite differences read as a possible mix-up of codes; recount levels on the unexplained part and a recount list ordered by money; the stop when the books' figures cannot be narrowed to the locations counted; and the actions for the approver, each with a blank approval line, kept apart from anything still waiting for a recount. No third-party text is reused.
