# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. Its starting point is the cost-of-goods method in finsilabs/awesome-ecommerce-skills (MIT). From the method itself it takes counting the cost of bringing a shipment in as part of what each unit costs, before any of it sells; giving each product a part of those charges in proportion to its share of the shipment's value, then dividing by its units; leaving a sales tax the business gets back out of cost; the warning that a product with no cost entered looks like all profit in a store's reports; and weighted average as a way to keep a running cost. From the review cases published with it, it takes sharing a charge by weight, volume or units as well as by value; never placing one shipment's charges twice; turning each product's part into a cost per unit; setting actual cost against an expected cost, marked above or below and listed from the biggest gap down; and the reminder that those charges come on bills of their own, sometimes long after the stock is on the shelf. Fully Powered AI designed the rest: set export headers with a stop on any mismatch; the check of units received against units invoiced; exchange rates used only as the person gives them; five types of charge, with a stop on any line that fits none; duty taken product by product from the customs entry exactly as entered, counted once when the broker passes it on, and every customs question written up for the broker and never answered; a method chosen per type of charge; shares rounded to the cent with any remainder on the product with the largest share and a rounding check against the shipment total; the recorded cost as the expected cost, with a percentage flag; the margin at both costs against the owner's floor; a new version when a late invoice arrives; and the draft list of costs to enter, with the line for the books and an approval line. No third-party text is reused.
