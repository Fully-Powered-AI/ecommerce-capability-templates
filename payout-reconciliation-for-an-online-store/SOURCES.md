# Sources and credits

Written by Fully Powered AI in its own words. The shape of the work draws on two published methods in finsilabs/awesome-ecommerce-skills (MIT). Its payment reconciliation method contributed setting each payout against both the orders inside it and the deposit it became; the point that a payout falls short of its sales because fees, refunds, chargebacks and money held in reserve are taken out first; a chargeback that costs its own amount and a separate fee; checking a processor's fees against the agreed rate as a task of its own; not treating recent sales that have yet to settle as faults; leaving amounts in the currency they were charged in; and leaving every exception for a person to settle. The review cases published with that method contributed pairing records on an exact amount inside a window of days, and a tolerance expressed in cents, not as a share of the amount. Its marketplace fee method contributed comparing each deduction with the channel's own fee table and keeping that table current, grouping fees by kind, holding advertising apart from the cost of selling, giving fees as a share of sales, recording gross sales and fees rather than the sum paid out, and the point that an overcharged fee can be claimed back. The review cases published with that method also carry gross sales, not the payout, as the revenue figure, and a fee rate that is not worked out when there are no sales. Fully Powered AI added the single monthly pack across every processor and marketplace, worked from exports with named column headings and a stop when one does not match; the check that every payout adds up from its own lines; a waiting period for each channel that separates an order still on its way from one that is missing; the possible link between two differences that is never treated as a match; and the figures for the entries and the list of fee differences, each prepared for a person to act on. No third-party text is reused.
